<p class="closer">We take almost everything in a house, garage, yard, or storage unit: furniture, mattresses, appliances, hot tubs, e-waste, exercise equipment, and small demo debris. A short list of items needs a licensed facility, and we leave those with you.</p>
<div class="answers">
<h3>Paint, solvents, and pesticides</h3>
<p class="closer">These are liquid hazardous waste and can't go to a standard dump.</p>
<h3>Propane tanks</h3>
<p class="closer">They are pressurized and need a hazardous waste drop-off or a tank exchange.</p>
<h3>Batteries</h3>
<p class="closer">Car and lithium batteries carry fire and chemical risk and need a hazardous waste drop-off.</p>
<h3>Tires</h3>
<p class="closer">Tires go to tire recyclers, not to a general dump.</p>
<h3>Asbestos and lead material</h3>
<p class="closer">Only licensed abatement crews can remove it. If a demo job turns up either one, stop work and call one.</p>
<h3>Medical waste</h3>
<p class="closer">Needles and biohazards need a licensed medical waste handler.</p>
<h3>Ammunition</h3>
<p class="closer">Contact your local police department to arrange safe disposal.</p>
</div>
<p class="closer">For paint, solvents, pesticides, and batteries, the City of Los Angeles runs household hazardous waste drop-offs called S.A.F.E. centers. Search for an LA S.A.F.E. center near you for locations and hours.</p>
